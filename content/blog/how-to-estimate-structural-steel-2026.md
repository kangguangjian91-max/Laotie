# How to Estimate Structural Steel: A Step-by-Step Guide for 2026

To estimate structural steel for a building, multiply the floor area by a typical steel weight factor (25-40 kg/m² for a standard warehouse), then multiply the total tonnage by the fabricated steel price per ton ($900-$1,500 in 2026), then add shipping, foundations, cladding and erection. This guide walks through each step with a worked example.

## Step 1: Define the Building Geometry

Start by writing down the basic dimensions of your project:

- **Length and width** (to calculate the footprint area)
- **Eave height** (standard warehouse: 6-10 m)
- **Clear span** (distance between columns)
- **Roof type** (single slope, gable, or flat)
- **Number of bays and columns**

These parameters determine the *structural form* — a simple portal frame for spans up to 40 m, a multi-span frame for wider layouts, or a space frame for column-free spans above 60 m. The structural form drives your steel weight factor.

## Step 2: Calculate the Floor Area

The floor area is the single biggest driver of steel quantity.

```
Floor area (m²) = Length (m) × Width (m)
```

Example: a 50 m × 20 m warehouse = **1,000 m²**.

For multi-storey buildings, multiply each floor area separately — a mezzanine or upper floor adds its own steel weight on top of the main frame.

## Step 3: Apply a Steel Weight Factor (kg/m²)

This is where experience matters. Steel weight per square meter varies by building type, span, and loading. Use these 2026 reference factors:

| Building Type | Clear Span | Steel Weight (kg/m²) |
|---------------|------------|----------------------|
| Warehouse / workshop, no crane | 20-30 m | 25-35 |
| Warehouse with 5-10t crane | 20-30 m | 35-45 |
| Industrial factory, 10-20t crane | 25-40 m | 45-60 |
| Mezzanine / multi-storey addition | — | 50-80 |
| Space frame roof (60m+ span) | 60-120 m | 30-55 |
| Aircraft hangar | 40-100 m | 40-65 |

> **Rule of thumb**: heavier cranes, wider spans, higher wind/snow loads, and taller eaves all push the weight factor upward. If your site is in a hurricane or heavy snow zone, add 10-20%.

**Calculate the tonnage:**

```
Steel tonnage (t) = Floor area (m²) × Weight factor (kg/m²) ÷ 1,000
```

Example (1,000 m² warehouse, 30 kg/m²):
```
1,000 × 30 ÷ 1,000 = 30 tons of structural steel
```

## Step 4: Price the Tonnage

The 2026 fabricated steel price (cut, drilled, welded, shot-blasted and painted) typically runs:

| Market | Fabricated Steel ($/ton) |
|--------|--------------------------|
| China FOB export | $900 - $1,200 |
| Southeast Asia | $950 - $1,300 |
| North America / Europe | $1,100 - $1,500 |
| Australia / Middle East | $1,150 - $1,550 |

**Fabricated cost:**

```
Steel cost = Tonnage × Fabricated price per ton
```

Example: 30 tons × $1,000/ton = **$30,000** (≈ $30/m²) for the steel frame only.

## Step 5: Add the Other Cost Lines

The steel frame is usually only **30-40% of the total project budget**. A complete estimate needs these lines:

| Cost Component | Typical Range | Notes |
|----------------|---------------|-------|
| Structural steel frame (fabricated) | $900-1,500 /ton | From Step 4 |
| Cladding (roof + walls) | $15-30 /m² | Sandwich panels cost more than single skin |
| Foundation | $15-40 /m² | Local contractor, excludes from FOB price |
| Erection / installation | $10-25 /m² | Or 5-8% of building cost |
| Shipping & customs | $5-20 /m² | Varies heavily by region |
| Accessories (doors, gutters, insulation) | $5-25 /m² | Crane and mezzanine are the big extras |

**Rule of thumb**: a complete turnkey steel warehouse typically lands at **$60-150 per m²** depending on region and specification — roughly 2.5-3.5× the bare frame cost.

## Step 6: Sanity-Check with a Second Method

Always cross-check your estimate using the **per-square-meter method**:

```
Frame cost /m² = Weight factor (kg/m²) × Fabricated price ($/ton) ÷ 1,000
```

For the worked example: 30 kg/m² × $1,000/ton ÷ 1,000 = **$30/m²**. This matches Step 4 — if the two methods disagree by more than 15%, re-check your weight factor or your tonnage price.

## Worked Example: 1,000 m² Warehouse Estimate

Let's pull the whole calculation together for a 50 m × 20 m portal-frame warehouse in the Philippines:

| Line | Calculation | Amount |
|------|-------------|--------|
| Floor area | 50 × 20 | 1,000 m² |
| Weight factor | standard warehouse | 30 kg/m² |
| Steel tonnage | 1,000 × 30 ÷ 1,000 | 30 t |
| Frame price | 30 t × $950/t (Southeast Asia) | $28,500 |
| Cladding | 1,000 × $20/m² | $20,000 |
| Shipping to Manila | 1,000 × $12/m² | $12,000 |
| Foundation & erection (local) | 1,000 × $30/m² | $30,000 |
| **Total installed** | | **$90,500 ≈ $90/m²** |

This matches the typical turnkey range for Southeast Asia. A bare FOB frame quotation would come back around **$28-30/m²** — which is why comparing quotes on a per-m² basis (rather than per-ton) is the most reliable way to evaluate suppliers.

## Common Mistakes When Estimating Structural Steel

1. **Ignoring secondary steel** — purlins, girts, bracing and cleats add 15-25% on top of primary frame weight. Many buyers estimate only the primary frame and under-budget by a fifth.
2. **Using an average tonnage price for everything** — a space frame costs more per ton than a simple portal frame because of fabrication complexity. Use the structure-appropriate price.
3. **Forgetting local additions** — foundation, erection, customs and inland transport are not part of the FOB steel price. Include them or the budget will overrun.
4. **Not cross-checking the two methods** — if your weight-factor math and your per-m² math disagree, one of your inputs is wrong.
5. **Underestimating wind/snow regions** — a 15 m eave or a cyclone-rated roof can raise steel weight by 10-20%.
