Floor deck is one of the most critical — yet most overlooked — components of a steel structure building. Whether you're building a multi-story factory, a warehouse with a mezzanine, or a commercial complex, the right floor deck system determines load capacity, construction speed, and long-term durability.

## What Is a Steel Structure Floor Deck?

A steel floor deck (also called **steel decking**, **metal decking**, or **composite deck**) is a cold-formed steel sheet used as a permanent formwork and structural component in concrete slab systems. It serves two purposes:

1. **Permanent formwork**: Eliminates the need for traditional timber or plywood formwork, saving labor and material costs.
2. **Composite reinforcement**: Once concrete hardens, the embossed steel deck bonds with the concrete to act as a composite slab, increasing strength and reducing deflection.

## Three Main Types of Steel Floor Deck

### 1. Open Floor Deck (Open Decking)

Open floor deck features ribs with open sides, allowing concrete to flow through and bond directly with the steel. It's the most common type for general construction.

| Specification | Typical Value |
|---------------|---------------|
| Steel thickness | 0.8–1.2 mm |
| Rib height | 38–76 mm |
| Rib spacing | 150–300 mm |
| Sheet width | 600–1,000 mm |
| Effective width | 500–915 mm |

**Best for**: Standard warehouses, mezzanine floors, commercial buildings

**Advantages**:
- Lower material cost
- Fast installation (no additional reinforcement needed in many cases)
- Good composite action with concrete

**Limitations**:
- Lower fire resistance compared to closed deck
- May require additional reinforcement for heavy loads

### 2. Closed Floor Deck (Closed Decking / Flat Bottom Deck)

Closed floor deck has a flat bottom with enclosed ribs, creating a smooth ceiling surface. This eliminates the need for a separate ceiling finish.

| Specification | Typical Value |
|---------------|---------------|
| Steel thickness | 0.9–1.5 mm |
| Rib height | 50–100 mm |
| Effective width | 600–900 mm |
| Bottom surface | Smooth (no exposed ribs) |

**Best for**: Office buildings, showrooms, clean rooms, multi-story parking

**Advantages**:
- Smooth ceiling finish — no additional plasterboard needed
- Better fire resistance (enclosed ribs act as fire barriers)
- Higher load capacity for the same steel thickness
- Excellent for acoustic treatment

**Limitations**:
- 15–25% higher material cost than open deck
- Slightly heavier per square meter

### 3. Composite Floor Deck (Deep Profile Deck)

Composite floor deck is a deep-ribbed profile designed specifically for maximum composite action with concrete. The embossments and interlocking ribs create a mechanical bond that makes the steel and concrete act as a single unit.

| Specification | Typical Value |
|---------------|---------------|
| Steel thickness | 0.9–1.5 mm |
| Rib height | 50–200 mm |
| Effective width | 600–900 mm |
| Concrete thickness | 120–250 mm |

**Best for**: Heavy industrial floors, multi-story factories, long-span mezzanines, parking structures

**Advantages**:
- Highest load capacity per unit weight
- Longest spans without temporary propping
- Reduces overall steel weight of the building frame
- Can support point loads up to 10+ tons per square meter

**Limitations**:
- Requires thicker concrete topping (higher concrete cost)
- Longer curing time before loading

## Floor Deck Installation Best Practices

1. **Propping**: For spans over 3.0 m, use temporary props at mid-span until concrete reaches 75% strength.
2. **Shear studs**: Composite decks require welded shear studs at 150–300 mm spacing for full composite action.
3. **Edge closures**: Install edge closure angles to prevent concrete spillage and ensure clean edges.
4. **Vapor barrier**: For ground-floor slabs, always install a polyethylene vapor barrier before the concrete pour.
5. **Curing**: Keep concrete moist for 7 days minimum. Do not apply heavy loads until 28-day strength is achieved.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can floor deck be used without concrete?**
A: Yes, but only as a permanent formwork for lightweight applications. For structural load-bearing, concrete topping is required.

**Q: What is the fire rating of steel floor deck?**
A: Bare steel deck has minimal fire resistance. With concrete topping, ratings of 1–2 hours can be achieved. Closed deck with fire protection can reach 3+ hours.

**Q: How much does floor deck weigh per square meter?**
A: 0.8 mm deck ≈ 8–10 kg/m²; 1.2 mm deck ≈ 12–15 kg/m². Total slab weight (deck + concrete) ranges from 250–400 kg/m².
